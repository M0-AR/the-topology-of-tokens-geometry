01 · The 30-second version

Every large language model converts text into tokens before it thinks. The tokenizer decides how many tokens that is. More tokens means more compute, more memory, more latency, and a bigger bill. Most teams pick a tokenizer by habit, not by measurement.

TopoBPE is a controlled, pre-registered benchmark that asks whether a sophisticated description of a tokenizer's shape — persistent homology — predicts its economics better than simple, cheap statistics. On 36 tokenizers, 6 languages, and 36 trained language models, it does not. The simple baseline explains 97.4% of held-out compression variance; adding 72 topology features drops that to 84.3% and fails the permutation test. One exploratory association survives correction, with a confidence interval that spans zero. The honest conclusion is the valuable one: measure the basics, skip the expensive geometry, and spend the savings elsewhere.

02 · Why this matters

Tokens are the unit economics of AI. Tokenizers set the exchange rate.

The hidden tax on every prompt

If your workload processes one billion bytes of text per month, a tokenizer that emits 4.0 bytes per token needs 250 million tokens. One that emits 3.5 needs 286 million — 14% more tokens for the same content.

At $0.50 per million input tokens, that difference is $18,000 a year on a single workload. At frontier-model prices, it is a line item a CFO will ask about. Yet tokenizer selection is usually inherited from a base model or chosen by leaderboard vibes.

  • No public benchmark separated tokenizer quality from model quality.
  • Topology tooling is expensive to build and hard to justify without evidence.

What this repository gives you

A frozen protocol, a reproducible pipeline, 36 trained tokenizers, 36 language-model runs, 80 measured features per tokenizer, and every result file needed to defend the conclusion in a technical or budget review.

It is three things at once: a benchmark, a cost calculator, and a teaching tool that takes a newcomer from “what is a token?” to reading a partial Spearman correlation with confidence.

  • Use the measured curves to choose a vocabulary size for your corpus.
  • Use the negative result to avoid buying complexity that does not predict.
  • Reproduce every number in Docker, down to the hashed manifest.
